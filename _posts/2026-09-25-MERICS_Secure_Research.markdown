**By Jessica Batke, Katja Drinhausen, and Daria Impiombato**

**This report was published by MERICS. The full report is available on the [MERICS website](https://merics.org/en/report/secure-research-and-china-lessons-community-experiences).**

<hr class="page__divider">

## Key takeaways

<ul><li><strong>Research in and on China is increasingly risky.</strong> This research is now more important than ever, but the current international geopolitical environment and national security considerations in China add a growing array of risks.</li><li><strong>Risks arise across a wide array of research fields. </strong>Those who conduct research on China in fields such as political science, international relations, history, economics, and culture encounter many barriers to carrying out meaningful exchanges or field work</li><li><strong>It can be difficult to see the risks.</strong> China produces world-class talent and innovation, especially in science, technology, engineering, and mathematics (STEM) fields. Given heightened attention to scholarly exchange with China in these fields, individuals and institutions whose work is not China-focused, but who work in China, on China, or with Chinese counterparts, need to nurture China literacy and consider the potential consequences of their interactions.</li><li><strong>Institutions should provide support and resources to their staff and to counterparts based in China.</strong> Rather than leaving individuals with the burden of navigating research safety and ethics questions, institutional policies should remain flexible and responsive to individual cases, helping researchers manage situations in which there are no optimal solutions.</li><li><strong>Individuals should take a pragmatic yet principled approach to research and exchanges. </strong>They should ensure they have the backing of thei institutions but also take individual steps to further enhance their safety and the quality of their work.</li><li><strong>Conducting work solely outside of China doesn’t mitigate all risk. </strong>Evaluate changing Chinese regulations, degraded access to data, and cybersecurity practices as you conduct open-source research work.</li></ul>
